Aditya Azad is a Ph.D. student in Robotics Engineering at Worcester Polytechnic Institute, where he joined the Aerial-Robot Control and Perception (ACP) Lab in August 2025. His research focuses on learning-based navigation for quadrotors that keeps the vehicle's motion aligned with what its onboard camera can see, using differentiable physics to train policies that fly safely through cluttered environments, and extends to multi-robot collaboration, perception, and sensor fusion. He is the lead author of [ClearNav](../../preprint/azad-2026-clearnav/), a perception-aligned quadrotor navigation policy trained through differentiable physics.

Before WPI, he earned his M.S. in Computer Science from New York University in 2025. At NYU he worked in the Agile Robotics and Perception Lab, first maintaining the lab's Docker and Singularity software pipeline and then conducting multi-robot research, including real-world multi-robot experiments, and in the Building Informatics and Visualization Lab, where he fine-tuned the Segment Anything Model and trained generative adversarial networks to detect and segment defects on building facades, work that took third place at the 2024 NYU Tandon Research Excellence Exhibit. He holds a B.S. in Computer Science from Purdue University (2022), where he was an undergraduate teaching assistant for systems programming. His earlier experience includes a software engineering internship at Cummins, building geographic data applications for the Central Road Research Institute of India, and open-source contributions to MindsDB.

Outside the lab he enjoys hiking, reading, painting, and building software.
